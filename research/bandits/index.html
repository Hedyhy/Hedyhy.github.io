Capstone Project · MA4198

Bandit Learning: Foundations and Thompson Sampling Proof Techniques

Author: Yu Hanyue
Supervisor: Prof. TAN Yan Fu, Vincent
Department of Mathematics, National University of Singapore — Honours Year Project, Semester 2, AY2025/2026

Summary

This report explores the theoretical and mathematical foundations of Multi-Armed Bandit (MAB) algorithms, focusing on the fundamental trade-off between exploration and exploitation. It establishes the measure-theoretic groundwork and systematically reviews canonical algorithms, including ETC and various UCB extensions (such as MOSS and KL-UCB), alongside their corresponding theoretical lower bounds, before providing an in-depth theoretical exposition of Thompson Sampling (TS). The primary scope of the latter half is to rigorously dissect, reconstruct, and compare two distinct methodologies for deriving logarithmic regret bounds for TS: the worst-case frequentist analysis and the symmetry-based Bayesian approach.

Independent contributions include a comparative visual analysis of UCB-type algorithms (Section 2.2), a rigorous derivation of the minimax lower bound Ω(√kn) via both the Bretagnolle–Huber construction and an alternative formulation from Pinsker's inequality (Section 2.3.2), a fully substantiated reconstruction of the Bayesian regret proof for Thompson Sampling that fills in technical steps left as exercises in the referenced textbook (Section 3.2.2), and a comparative synthesis of the Agrawal–Goyal and Lattimore–Szepesvári proof techniques that unifies their perspectives on how Thompson Sampling achieves robustness across analytical frameworks (Section 3.2.3).
